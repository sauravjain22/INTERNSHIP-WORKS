Subject: Data Requirements and Approach for Customer Churn Analysis

Hi Estelle,

Based on our discussion, I think we should investigate multiple possible reasons why customers may choose to stay with or switch energy providers, rather than assuming price is the only factor.

Some possible factors include:
- Pricing and changes in energy costs
- Customer service and overall customer experience
- Type and source of energy offered
- Availability and reliability of the service
- Contract type and contract length
- Competitor offers and pricing
- Customer location and business characteristics

To investigate these factors, I think we will need data such as:
- Customer purchase/consumption history
- Energy prices and price changes over time
- Customer tenure and contract information
- Customer location
- Customer type/business characteristics
- Customer service interactions or complaints
- Information about the customer's energy source or plan
- Customer churn status and, where available, the date or timing of churn

Once we receive the data, I would follow the five-step data science approach.

First, I will clean and validate the data, including checking for missing values, incorrect data, duplicates, and inconsistencies. As the saying goes, "garbage in, garbage out," so data quality is important before starting the analysis.

Second, I will perform exploratory data analysis to understand the data and identify patterns or relationships between customer characteristics, pricing, and churn. I will use appropriate visualizations and statistical analysis to investigate the initial hypotheses.

Third, I will perform feature engineering to create useful variables that may help explain or predict customer churn.

Fourth, I will build and evaluate predictive models to identify which factors are most strongly associated with churn and test whether price sensitivity is actually a critical factor.

Finally, I will interpret the results and communicate the key insights and recommendations in a way that is useful for PowerCo's business decisions.

This approach should allow us to test the price-sensitivity hypothesis while also considering other potential drivers of customer churn.
